01 / The product

A webinar is never just a webinar.

Behind every live session is a chain of decisions: what to create, which audience to invite, how to deliver the event, and what to do after it ends.

EasyWebinar brings those responsibilities together. My design focus was making its breadth feel navigable—so hosts can recognize their next action without losing the surrounding event context.

Problem

Many related tools compete for attention across creation, setup, delivery and follow-up.

My role

Information architecture, interaction design, dashboards, AI-assisted workflows and reusable UI foundations.

Intent

Help hosts begin confidently, return to unfinished work and review the outcome with less friction.

02 / The challenge

Problem framing based on the design work and representative host scenarios.

When everything is possible, what comes next needs to be obvious.

A host shouldn't need to mentally reconstruct an entire workflow every time they open the product. The challenge was to give different tasks clear places, while keeping the experience connected.

05 / Information architecture

The product-wide sidebar answers where am I going? The individual webinar workspace answers what am I doing inside this event? Separating those levels avoids mixing global destinations with event-specific settings.

08 / Decisions and trade-offs

Every decision has a reason.

Selected interface decisions that balance capability with clarity. Expand each note to understand the reasoning rather than seeing only the final screen.

Why put an overview before detailed settings?
The decision

Starting from a summary restores context for hosts who return to an existing event. It surfaces the schedule, pricing, people and essential information before they enter a specialized area.

The trade-off

More navigation destinations remain visible, but each category has a recognizable responsibility.

Why use repeated rows for communications?
The decision

Aligned structures make messages comparable. Timing, subject, audience and controls can be scanned without re-learning each card.

The trade-off

Individual messages have less room for expressive layouts; a deeper editing surface handles specialized configuration.

Why keep violet as an accent rather than a canvas?
The decision

White and near-white surfaces prioritize dense information and black navigation provides a stable edge. Violet is reserved for interactive emphasis, selection and important calls to action.

The trade-off

The experience is less visually loud, but hierarchy stays consistent across many operational screens.

12 / Reflection

Make the complex feel connected.

The work aimed to make a broad webinar product feel like one continuous experience—from choosing a format to preparing content, running an event and reviewing its performance.

Across the workspace, the design principle stayed consistent: give each task a clear home, preserve context, and use familiar patterns so the next action remains visible.

What I would validate next

How quickly can a returning host identify their next preparation task? Can they understand what remains incomplete before publishing? Can they move between AI-assisted creation and manual refinement without losing track of assets?

The portfolio presents design rationale and example product screens. It does not claim unverified customer metrics, completed usability studies or conversion improvements.
