01 / Product intent

A community built
around doing more together.

GoodPaw brings nearby dog discovery, pet-and-parent profiles, friendship, messaging, and pet care into one mobile experience. The product centers on the relationship between pets and the people who care for them.

My organizing decision was to connect three needs: finding a suitable companion, building familiarity, and supporting everyday care. Each has a place in the app without forcing every interaction into a single feed.

01

Find each other.

A nearby map and pet profiles create a starting point for discovery.

02

Build familiarity.

Friendship, conversations, and everyday Moments extend the relationship.

03

Support the routine.

Care records and requests to trusted connections give the product practical value.

This case study explains decisions evidenced in the supplied app captures and UI reference. It does not claim user interviews, testing results, or post-launch performance that have not been provided.

The problem / From nearby to familiar

Being close doesn’t mean
knowing who to meet.

Pet parents need more than a pin on a map. They need enough context to consider a companion, a mutual way to connect, and a place to coordinate the next step.

For the parent

Confidence before contact.

Understand the pet and the person behind the profile before starting a conversation.

For the pet

A suitable companion.

Make characteristics and play preferences useful alongside proximity.

For everyday life

A reason to stay connected.

Support familiarity through Moments and practical routines through Pet Care.

Design goal

Help pet parents move from discovering nearby dogs to building a familiar, mutually accepted connection.

The product question:
How can nearby discovery become
a relationship worth returning to?

01

Nearby.
Without being exact.

The map makes proximity tangible. Dog clusters, park markers, a radius control, and a draggable results drawer give discovery a spatial starting point.

Make the privacy boundary visible.

The interface states that locations are approximate beside the discovery controls. This puts the boundary where the location decision happens.

Pair exploration with browsing.

The results drawer provides a list-oriented route from the same map. Users can explore an area and then move into nearby profiles.

Give discovery an explicit state.

“Discovery on” makes visibility a recognizable setting rather than an invisible assumption.

02 / From discovery to connection

A pet’s profile.
A person behind it.

I paired pet identity with parent context, then connected that profile to friendship and messaging. This gives the path from browsing to a conversation a clear structure.

03 / Everyday community

Little moments.
Less pressure.

Moments gives everyday pet activity its own destination. The composer puts the photo first, follows with a short caption, and offers optional activity tags.

Make the sharing boundary explicit.

The captured composer explains the 24-hour lifetime and approximate location before a post is shared.

Keep the first contribution approachable.

A quiet empty state invites a first post rather than presenting an unexplained blank feed.

Separate community from coordination.

Moments, Friends, and Messages have different navigation destinations, giving public sharing and direct conversations distinct contexts.

These captures show an earlier nearby-sharing configuration. They document the flow shown here, rather than asserting every state of the current app.

04 / Everyday utility

Connection is social.
Care is practical.

I grouped health records, activity, and reminders under the pet’s care space. The dashboard provides recognizable entry points without putting every record into the profile itself.

Care Circle extends that space to trusted connections. The request is addressed to one person, with a defined time window and type of help.

Some supplied captures use “Alex’s Care.” The case study uses the current feature name, Pet Care; the original screenshots remain intact.

The product boundary: trusted help, not an open marketplace.

The supplied care flow asks the parent to choose an existing connection. Handover details are kept out of the structured form, with private coordination encouraged instead.

06 / Reflection & next questions

A reason to meet.
A reason to return.

GoodPaw’s core design connects discovery to an ongoing relationship. Profiles establish context, conversations support coordination, Moments encourage familiarity, and Pet Care adds everyday utility.

What I would validate next

Can new parents understand approximate location? Can they distinguish a friend request from an accepted connection? Does the path from chat to playdate feel clear? Is requesting care from one trusted person understandable?

These are proposed evaluation questions, not reported test findings. Research methods, historical iterations, and measured outcomes can be added when documented.
