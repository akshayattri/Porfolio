The problem

A report can tell you what’s wrong.
Acting on it is harder.

How might I help a homeowner move from an inspection finding to a clear, traceable next step?

Homewise explores the gap between receiving a property report and deciding what to address. The design brings the property, observation, source evidence and follow-up into one connected workspace.

Research & framing

Start with the decisions.
Then design the screens.

I framed the concept around three questions a homeowner needs to answer. These are working hypotheses for an independent concept, ready to be tested with homebuyers and homeowners.

What needs my attention?

Organize findings by assigned priority and system so a reader can identify where to begin.

Why does it matter?

Keep the observation, image and original report reference close to the explanation.

What do I do next?

Turn a finding into an editable action with status and a due date.

Research scope · Scenario analysis and task mapping. The personas below are provisional. User interviews and usability testing are the next validation step.

Provisional user personas

Two moments in a home’s life.

Before moving in

The first-time buyer

Needs to understand an unfamiliar report and prepare questions for a qualified professional.

Design response

Plain-language explanations, visible priorities and direct access to the source finding.

After moving in

The hands-on homeowner

Needs to keep observations organized and track work across rooms and home systems.

Design response

Property-specific reports, room and system views, and actions that retain their finding context.

Product decisions

Context before complexity.

The property dashboard answers the immediate question of what needs attention, while preserving a route back to the full report.

Separate the home from the workspace

A global overview summarizes activity. Opening a property narrows the context to its report, systems and next steps.

Make priorities explainable

Priority labels sit alongside finding titles. The dashboard reflects saved report priorities, keeping the source of the ranking visible.

From observation to action

Keep the evidence attached.

A finding carries its location, system, observation and source. Creating a task from that finding prefills the context, while leaving the owner in control of the action.

Tasks are created deliberately. Recording a finding alone does not add work to the planner.

Trust & control

Review before relying.

The sample import journey includes a finding-by-finding review. Titles, locations, systems and priorities can be checked before confirming the report.

The assistant keeps questions tied to saved findings, with source links that lead back to the evidence.

Prototype scope · PDF extraction and assistant responses use a fictional sample dataset. Reports and actions created in the prototype are stored locally.

Reflection & next steps

A complete journey.
A clear plan to validate it.

The prototype connects property setup, report authoring, findings exploration and action tracking across desktop and mobile. The component library gives the experience a consistent foundation.

I would next test whether homeowners can identify a priority finding, explain it in their own words and create an appropriate follow-up task. Task completion, source-reference use and moments of hesitation would guide the next iteration.

Independent concept · The property and report data are fictional. Product impact and usability outcomes remain to be validated.
