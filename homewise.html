01 / Overview

From inspection report to informed action.

An inspection can surface dozens of issues, but it rarely tells a homeowner what to tackle first, which specialist to contact, or how to track progress. Homewise closes that gap with a calmer, more understandable workspace.

01

Understand

Translate technical findings into accessible language and clear property context.

02

Prioritize

Separate urgent safety concerns from routine maintenance and nice-to-have work.

03

Act

Turn findings into assigned, trackable tasks with notes, status, and next steps.

02 / Problem framing

A detailed report isn't always a useful plan.

“What does this finding mean, how urgent is it, and what should I do next?”

This was the guiding design question. The opportunity was not to replace professional inspectors or contractors, but to create a structured layer between understanding an inspection and taking action on it.

Challenge 01

Information overload

Long reports make it hard to distinguish critical findings from minor observations.

Challenge 02

Unclear ownership

Problems are described, but follow-up actions and responsibilities are fragmented.

Challenge 03

Lost progress

Homeowners need one place to organize work, decisions, and supporting details.

03 / Discovery

Research-informed assumptions, not invented findings.

I framed the concept around likely homeowner needs and the structure of inspection reports, then mapped assumptions that would need validation before a production launch.

First-time buyer

Provisional persona · needs reassurance and clarity

Wants to understand unfamiliar inspection terminology, identify what could affect a purchase decision, and prepare informed questions for professionals.

Existing homeowner

Provisional persona · needs ongoing organization

Wants to turn an inspection or maintenance list into manageable work, track what is complete, and keep property history accessible.

These are design hypotheses and illustrative personas, not claims of completed interviews, surveys, usability tests, or measured customer outcomes.

05 / Visual identity

Quiet by design. Precise where it matters.

07 / Design decisions

Reducing decisions, not removing detail.

Hierarchy

Priority before volume

Lead with key findings and next actions instead of presenting an unfiltered report.

Trust

Keep source context

Connect plain-language summaries to original inspection details for review.

Continuity

Bridge insight to action

Let users create and track tasks from findings without re-entering information.

The next stage would validate terminology, prioritization, and report-import comprehension with homeowners and inspection professionals. No conversion, task completion, or usability metrics are claimed for this concept.
