Prologue / The design intent

Let the story lead.
Let the interface step back.

Luma brings an illustrated library, chapter-based reading, personal book states, and interactive stories into one mobile app. The design needs to welcome readers into a fictional world without making the controls part of the distraction.

My core design direction separates two layers: expressive artwork for entering a story, and a quieter structure for reading and finding the next action.

Atmosphere creates the invitation.

Large illustrated covers establish a story’s mood before the reader commits. Dark surfaces carry that mood into the surrounding product.

Structure makes it sustainable.

Chapters, reading preferences, bookmarks, and My Books provide the practical support needed to leave a story and return.

The following journey is grounded in the implemented interfaces and source references supplied with the kit. Historical research findings, early explorations, and measured outcomes have not been supplied.

Chapter 01 / Discovery

A visual invitation.
A clear next step.

I used story artwork as the discovery anchor, then paired it with metadata and chapter structure so readers can decide what to begin.

Chapter 02 / Reading comfort

Keep the atmosphere.
Protect the words.

I kept the reading surface visually quiet and gave prose a generous rhythm. Illustrated context supports the story; the text remains the foreground.

Give readers control.

The standard reader defaults to 21 px text with a 1.9 line height, with adjustable sizing from 17–27 px. Reading comfort is a setting the reader can shape.

Keep orientation close.

Chapter and section progress sit with reading navigation. Preferences and bookmarks are available from the reading context rather than requiring a return to the library.

The design tension is deliberate: rich imagery creates immersion, while the prose surface needs sufficient separation for sustained reading.

Chapter 03 / Continuity

Leaving the story
shouldn’t mean losing it.

My Books separates Reading, Saved, and Finished. This gives a reader’s intention and reading progress distinct places in the product.

Continue-reading cards surface the current chapter and remaining context. The design makes return a direct action, rather than another search through the full library.

Progress shown in these exports is seeded demo content, not evidence of real reader behavior.

Sometimes you follow the story.
Sometimes it asks you
what happens next.

Chapter 04 / Interactive storytelling

Read the scene.
Choose the action.

Luma’s interactive mode extends reading into participation. Illustrated chapter openings establish the world; decision scenes bring the reader to a specific action.

Keep choices in the story’s language.

The decision interface uses descriptive options and supporting context. The next action is expressed as a character decision, rather than a detached navigation command.

Selected screens avoid ending reveals. Multiple-ending support is documented in the kit, while this case study concentrates on the interaction design.

The interaction loop

A decision. A discovery.
A memory of the journey.

Interactive reading needs more than choices: it needs clear feedback and a way to recall what the reader has learned.

A boundary worth keeping

Show the path taken.
Keep the unknown unknown.

The companion is limited to discoveries from the current journey. The journey view records visited scenes and choices while keeping unvisited scenes and other endings hidden.

This supports recollection without turning the story into a map of spoilers. The reader can revisit what they know without being shown the experience they have not had.

Epilogue / Reflection

Immersion is the invitation.
Clarity keeps it open.

Luma brings discovery, comfortable reading, continuity, and interactive storytelling into a shared visual world. The design keeps the story expressive while making navigation and actions predictable.

What I would evaluate next

Can readers find a suitable story and understand its length? Are reading controls easy to discover without interrupting the flow? Do clue confirmations and journal categories help recall? Do choice descriptions communicate intent clearly?

These are proposed evaluation questions. No testing results or retention improvements are claimed.
