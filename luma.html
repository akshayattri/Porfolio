Prologue / The design intent

Let the story lead.
Let the interface step back.

Luma brings an illustrated library, chapter-based reading, personal book states, and interactive stories into one mobile app. The design needs to welcome readers into a fictional world without making the controls part of the distraction.

My core design direction separates two layers: expressive artwork for entering a story, and a quieter structure for reading and finding the next action.

Discovery / The first decision

Invite with the artwork.
Orient with the details.

Illustrated covers establish mood. Genre, reading time, and a chapter list explain the commitment before a reader begins. Home, Library, Book Overview, and My Books appear together in the screen anthology below.

Chapter 02 / Reading comfort

Keep the atmosphere.
Protect the words.

I kept the reading surface visually quiet and gave prose a generous rhythm. Illustrated context supports the story; the text remains the foreground.

Give readers control.

The standard reader defaults to 21 px text with a 1.9 line height, with adjustable sizing from 17–27 px. Reading comfort is a setting the reader can shape.

Keep orientation close.

Chapter and section progress sit with reading navigation. Preferences and bookmarks are available from the reading context rather than requiring a return to the library.

The design tension is deliberate: rich imagery creates immersion, while the prose surface needs sufficient separation for sustained reading.

Make returning direct.

My Books separates Reading, Saved, and Finished, with progress attached to the story.

Chapter 04 / Interactive storytelling

Read the scene.
Choose the action.

Luma’s interactive mode extends reading into participation. Illustrated chapter openings establish the world; decision scenes bring the reader to a specific action.

Keep choices in the story’s language.

The decision interface uses descriptive options and supporting context. The next action is expressed as a character decision, rather than a detached navigation command.

The interaction loop

A decision. A discovery.
A memory of the journey.

Interactive reading needs more than choices: it needs clear feedback and a way to recall what the reader has learned.

A decision worth protecting

Recall the journey without revealing the ending.

The companion groups people, places, and clues from the reader’s current path. Visited scenes support recall while unvisited scenes and other endings stay hidden.
